```
They're right about the email.

There's a post going around on how to get job offers. Its advice: stop relying on applications, catch hiring posts while they're fresh, and email the person who posted the job. Keep it plain, because "short, direct, slightly messy emails stand out."

I agree. But it leaves out the hard part: which person to write to, and what to say.

Every job has four people who decide whether you get it. Each one needs a different note.

The hiring manager feels the empty seat. Name one problem their team has (the posting usually tells you), add one line of proof you can help, and ask one easy question.

Their boss approved the role. Say why their team's goal is the one you want to work on, and keep it shorter.

Someone on the team knows what the job really needs. Ask one real question about the work. Not for a referral, not yet.

The recruiter runs the process. Say you applied and why: the role, one line on fit, and when you're free to talk.

Four people, four short notes, each in your own words.

That's the method I used to land jobs at ClickUp, Deel and Google, and it's what we built Day60 to run: it finds the four people for one job and drafts a first note to each. You rewrite it until it sounds like you, then you send it.

Being good at the work still matters most. This just gets the right people to see it.

Pick one job you want today and find its four people.

Who will you write to first?

#jobsearch #networking #hiring
```
